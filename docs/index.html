0.01 — What it does

Save the video,
not just the link.

Most pages don't offer a download button. Video Downloader finds the video anyway — even when it streams in pieces or plays inside another site's player — and hands you one file.

01 · Finds every video

One click shows what's on the page

Open the page, click the toolbar icon, and see every video it found — including ones playing inside a player from another site, and ones that load after the page does.

02 · Works where you watch

Webinar, training, and lecture players

Built for the players work and school actually use — Vimeo, Wistia, Panopto, Vidyard, JW Player, Mux, Brightcove, ON24, Cloudflare Stream, and more. If you're signed in and can watch it, it can usually save it.

03 · Your choice

Pick the quality

Grab the full‑HD version, a smaller file to save space, or just the audio for listening on the go. Picture and sound are combined into one file automatically.

04 · Tidy files

Named after what it is

Files are named after the session or video title — Quarterly All‑Hands.mp4, not stream_8f3a.mpd.

05 · Slides too

Bring the deck along

If the page links the presentation — PDF, PowerPoint, or Keynote — it's listed right next to the video so you can save both.

06 · In your control

Pause, resume, keep going

See progress and time left. Pause a long download and pick it up later. If the connection hiccups it retries on its own, and a moment of missing video won't ruin a two‑hour recording.

07 · Private

Nothing leaves your browser

No account, no analytics, no tracking. The extension only talks to the site you're downloading from — never to us.

08 · Fair play

Respects copy protection

Movies and shows from services like Netflix or Disney+ are copy‑protected. The extension recognizes that and tells you, instead of trying to break it.

0.02 — How it works

Three steps from
watching to saved.

Nothing to set up or learn. Curious what happens behind the scenes? The FAQ has the technical details.

01

Open the video

Go to the page with the video. On some sites it helps to press play first, so the video starts loading.

02

Click the icon

Click Video Downloader in your toolbar to see each video on the page. For a video inside an embedded player, click Resolve Stream to find it.

03

Choose and save

Pick a quality and press Download. The file lands in your Downloads folder, ready to watch offline.

Use it the way it was built.

Video Downloader exists because legitimate video keeps getting harder to save — conferences that vanish, training portals that expire, courses that lock, journalism that gets taken down. It's for video you're allowed to access and have a reason to keep.

It doesn't try to break the copy protection used by services like Netflix, Disney+, or Apple TV — protected video is recognized and left alone. Respect copyright, the site's terms, and the people who made the video. When in doubt, ask permission.

0.05 — FAQ

Questions, answered.

Everyday questions first; the technical details follow.

What does Video Downloader do?

It saves videos from web pages as a single file on your computer, so you can keep them and watch offline — webinars, training sessions, lectures, conference talks, and videos playing inside embedded players. Click the toolbar icon, pick a video, and press Download.

Is it really free? Does it track me?

It's free, with no account, no ads, no analytics, and no tracking. Nothing about what you watch is sent anywhere — the extension only talks to the site you're downloading from, and everything else happens inside your browser. There is no server owned by the developer.

Which sites does it work with?

Most sites that play video right in the page, plus the embedded players common at work and school: Vimeo, Wistia, Panopto, Vidyard, JW Player, Mux, Brightcove, ON24, Cloudflare Stream, Microsoft Medius, and others. If you're signed in and can watch it, it can usually save it. YouTube isn't supported.

Can it download Netflix, Disney+, or other copy‑protected video?

No. Those services use copy protection (DRM such as Widevine, PlayReady, and FairPlay). Video Downloader recognizes it and tells you the video can't be saved — it never tries to get around it. It's meant for video you're allowed to access and have a reason to keep.

Which browsers does it work in?

Google Chrome and Microsoft Edge (version 114 or newer), plus Brave, Arc, Vivaldi, Opera, and other Chromium‑based browsers. Firefox and Safari aren't supported yet.

How do I install it?

Download the latest videodownloader-vX.Y.Z.zip from GitHub Releases and unzip it. Open chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode, click Load unpacked, and choose the unzipped folder. The Video Downloader icon appears in your toolbar. A Chrome Web Store listing is in review.

My video saved as a .ts file. How do I turn it into an MP4?

Some streams save as MPEG‑TS (.ts); most players, such as VLC, open them as‑is. Other streams, including DASH and fMP4/CMAF HLS, save straight to MP4. To convert a .ts file without losing quality, run ffmpeg -i input.ts -c copy output.mp4 — it repackages the file in seconds without re‑encoding.

Technical: which streaming formats are supported?

HLS (.m3u8) master and media playlists, including fMP4/CMAF playlists with #EXT-X-MAP init segments (saved as MP4) and #EXT-X-BYTERANGE segments fetched with HTTP Range requests. DASH (.mpd) with SegmentTemplate, SegmentTimeline, SegmentList, and SegmentBase — the chosen video representation and the best audio track are muxed into a single fragmented MP4 in the browser, with no re‑encoding and no ffmpeg. Direct files (MP4, WebM, and others) download as‑is. For live HLS, the segments currently in the playlist window are captured.

Technical: can it download AES‑128 encrypted HLS streams?

Yes. When a playlist declares #EXT-X-KEY METHOD=AES-128, the extension fetches the key, takes the IV from the playlist or derives it from the media sequence number, and decrypts each segment with AES‑CBC through the browser's Web Crypto API (crypto.subtle) — no WASM, no server. SAMPLE‑AES, FairPlay skd:// keys, PlayReady/Widevine KEYFORMATs, and Adobe Access (#EXT-X-FAXS-CM) are treated as DRM and refused.

Technical: how does it handle cross‑origin iframe embeds and sign‑ins?

The content script runs in every frame and classifies each <iframe> by domain (Medius, ON24, Vimeo, Mux, Wistia, Brightcove, JW Player, Cloudflare Stream, Vidyard, Panopto, and more) and by path (/embed/, /video/, /player/). For Vimeo, Wistia, JW Player, Mux, Cloudflare Stream, Vidyard, and Panopto it asks the platform's own public player API for the manifest — the approach the open‑source yt‑dlp project uses — fetched from inside the embed's frame so the platform sees its own cookies and origin. Otherwise it falls back to manifests captured from network traffic, then to parsing the embed page's HTML. JWT tokens from the iframe URL and session cookies (chrome.cookies) are forwarded on manifest, segment, and key requests; tokens stay in memory only.

Technical: how does it find videos on a page?

In layers: network interception (chrome.webRequest) of video MIME types and .m3u8/.mpd manifests; <video>, <source>, <object>, and <embed> elements; og:video (including extension‑less URLs typed by og:video:type), twitter:player:stream, and JSON‑LD VideoObject contentUrl/embedUrl; data-* attributes; media URLs in inline player setup code (JW Player, Video.js, Flowplayer) and framework data (Next.js __NEXT_DATA__, Nuxt); and <mux-player> elements. A MutationObserver re‑scans single‑page apps and lazy‑loaded players.

Technical: how does it cope with long or flaky downloads?

Segments download six at a time through a fetch‑strategy chain (page context, content script, then service worker). Each segment retries up to five times with exponential backoff capped at 20 seconds. A segment that stays unavailable is skipped and reported instead of failing the whole file — never the first segment or an init segment, and at most 10% of a download. When a CDN signs only the playlist URL, its query‑string token is carried onto segment and key requests. Anvato and Uplynk ad segments are dropped. Downloads can be paused, resumed, and cancelled.

Technical: how is it built?

A Chrome Manifest V3 extension in plain JavaScript ES modules — no build step, no npm dependencies. A content script detects media in every frame; the service worker intercepts network traffic, resolves embeds, parses HLS and DASH manifests, and orchestrates downloads; a segment downloader fetches, decrypts, and assembles; a small JS muxer combines DASH video and audio into one MP4; chrome.downloads saves the result. See ARCHITECTURE.md for the full design.
